A1

Alligator Traps

↑

Gobble up grounders with proper two-hand technique.

▶ Watch · 0:12

Setup

  • Line up team · coach stands 10 ft away
  • First player in set stance — knees bent, hips low

How to Play

  • Roll a grounder to the first fielder
  • They receive with both hands — top hand snaps down like an alligator
  • Throw back to coach
  • 3 grounders per player, then go to the back of the line
  • Track clean fields — challenge them to beat their score next round
Coaching tip Eyes on the ball all the way into the glove. The better they see it, the less likely it is to scoot past them.
A2

Base Path Blitz

↑

Every base-running scenario, on your call.

▶ Watch · 1:17

Setup

  • Line team up behind home plate
  • Coach in foul territory near a base path (a second coach can cover the other side)

How to Play

  • Player steps into the batter's box — no bat
  • On your call, they fake-swing and take off
  • Approaching 1st: you tell them run through · turn & look · round to 2nd
  • Approaching 2nd: you tell them stay · go to 3rd · round home
  • +1 every time they touch the plate
Coaching tip Runners don't watch the ball — they listen to the coach. Their job is run hard and react.
A3

Tee Time

↑

Turn weak contact into line drives.

▶ Watch · 2:50

Setup

  • Pairs line up outside the batting cage
  • Within each pair: one hitter, one feeder · helmets on
  • First pair steps to the tee inside the cage

How to Play

  • Feeder places ball on the tee, then steps a safe distance away
  • Hitter takes a controlled, level swing — drive a line drive through the box
  • Feeder reloads after each swing
  • When the bucket is empty, both retrieve balls and switch roles
Coaching tip Even Major Leaguers do tee work. Strip away pitch recognition so they can focus purely on balance, form, and finish — that's how a swing actually gets refined.
A4

Lazy Catch

↑

Read grounders from a low knees-down stance.

▶ Watch · 4:07

Setup

  • Groups of 5 · coach 15 ft from the front of the line
  • All 5 players on both knees (don't sit on heels)
  • Glove fingertips on the ground, ready

How to Play

  • Roll a grounder to each player in turn
  • They field & throw back without standing or sitting
  • Work down the line · then back, this time with backhanded grounders
  • +1 per clean field — beat last round's score
Coaching tip Two hands every time. Snap the top hand down on the ball like an alligator. Backhand plays = scoop & lift. Both hands is the only way to make it to the show.
A5

Belly Up

↑

Reaction time — react, charge, field.

▶ Watch · 5:25

Setup

  • Team lies face-down on their bellies in a line
  • Coach 20 ft away with a ball

How to Play

  • Call "Up!" and roll a ball toward the first player
  • They pop up, charge, field, throw back
  • Work down the line · mix in a few pop-ups to keep them honest
Coaching tip Don't let them jump the gun before you call up. First step in fielding is seeing where the ball is going.
A6

Up Top Down Low

↑

Hand-eye coordination — high & low glove positions.

▶ Watch · 6:30

Setup

  • Pair up players · ~10 ft apart
  • One ball per pair

How to Play

  • Round 1: underhand pop-ups · partner catches with glove in high-five position (fingers up)
  • 10 catches each, then switch to round 2
  • Round 2: low underhand throws · partner catches with glove in low-five position (fingers down)
  • Count total catches · beat the score next round
Coaching tip Every fly ball pros make look like "a can of corn" was built on reps like this. Drill the glove position now — it's muscle memory they'll lean on for years.
A7

Force Out · Tag Out

↑

Learn the two ways to erase a runner.

▶ Watch · 7:33

Setup

  • Pairs of 1 runner + 1 fielder (with glove & ball)
  • Spread across the base paths · pairs ~10 ft apart

How to Play

  • Coach calls one of two commands:
  • "Tag out!" → fielder tries to tag the runner
  • "Force out!" → fielder sprints to a bag & tags it with their foot before the runner gets there
  • +1 per successful out · 60 sec, then switch roles
Coaching tip Force out vs. tag out trips up every young player. Simplest cue: on the playground, tag means you're it. On the diamond, tag means you're out.
A8

Cleanup Crew

↑

Low-pressure way to get comfortable throwing overhand.

▶ Watch · 8:42

Setup

  • Cones form two adjoining rectangles — one on infield dirt, one on outfield grass
  • Equal number of practice balls in each zone
  • Split team in half · one team per zone

How to Play

  • On go, each team throws balls overhand into the other team's zone
  • After 1 minute call "Freeze!" — everyone drops what they have
  • Whichever team has fewer balls in their zone wins
Coaching tip Don't sweat the form yet — the chaos is the point. Just get the ball across. Refine throwing mechanics in another drill.
A9

Grab Bag

↑

Alternate grounders and pop-ups, line to line.

▶ Watch · 9:48

Setup

  • Two cones 10 ft apart in the outfield
  • Split team in half, line up behind each cone
  • Coach ~15 ft in front, between the lines, with a bucket

How to Play

  • Round 1: grounders to one line, pop-ups to the other
  • Field, throw back, move to the end of the other line
  • Round 2: mix grounders & pop-ups between both lines — they stay on their toes
  • +1 per clean field — beat the round-1 score
Coaching tip Foam balls tempt one-handed catches. Reinforce two hands every time — that's the habit that travels into real games and keeps them off the blooper reel.
A10

Blast Ball

↑

Low-stakes, high-fun scrimmage — first taste of the game.

▶ Watch · 11:12

Setup

  • Split team: one group at bat, one in the field (no gloves needed)
  • Coach pitches for both teams
  • Use a plastic bat & practice ball

How to Play (modified rules)

  • No strikeouts — hitter keeps swinging until they make contact
  • Innings end only when every hitter has batted
  • Any ball that reaches outfield grass = home run
  • Most runs after 3 innings = Blast Ball World Champions
Coaching tip Bigger ball + no strikeouts = newbies feel safe and successful. Crossing home plate is just as sweet — celebrate every run.
