A1

Tee Time

↑

Pure form work — line drives off the tee, no pitch pressure.

▶ Watch · 0:14

Setup

  • Pair players up · line pairs outside the batting cage
  • One tee in the cage · bucket of balls beside it
  • First pair in: helmets on · name a hitter and a feeder

How to Play

  • Feeder places a ball on the tee, then steps clear
  • Hitter takes a controlled, level swing — line drive through the middle
  • Repeat until the bucket is empty, then both shag the balls
  • Switch roles and run it back
Coaching tip If a player asks why no live pitching — tell them the pros still take tee work. This is where you fix balance, finish, and swing path with zero pitch-recognition noise.
A2

Force Fields

↑

Swing close to the fence to build a more compact, powerful cut.

▶ Watch · 2:42

Setup

  • Team lined up in the outfield, facing the wall
  • Draw 3 lines in the warning track: 3 ft, 5 ft, and 7 ft from the wall
  • One bat, one helmet up first

How to Play

  • Furthest line first — full normal swing
  • Middle line — pull hands in closer to the body
  • Closest line — hands tight, short and compact (don't hit the fence)
  • Rotate so every player gets several turns at all three distances
Coaching tip Long, open swings look powerful but are slower and weaker. A compact swing concentrates force on the ball — that's where real game-day power comes from.
A3

Kneeling Good

↑

Take the legs out so the hands do the work — one piece at a time.

▶ Watch · 4:00

Setup

  • Line team up in foul territory
  • Coach 4 ft to the side of home plate with a bucket
  • Hitter in box: back knee down, front leg extended for balance

How to Play (3 rounds)

  • Round 1 — bottom hand only · lead with the elbow
  • Round 2 — top hand only · emphasize follow-through
  • Round 3 — both hands · swing away
  • 3 clean contacts in each round before moving on
Coaching tip Not even the Bambino swung for the fences every pitch. Sometimes a single or a ground out gets the job done — kneeling exposes whose hands are doing the real work.
A4

Line Drive Derby

↑

Competitive tee work — most line drives in two minutes wins.

▶ Watch · 5:29

Setup

  • Two tees inside the cage, 10 ft apart
  • Two pairs at a time — one hitter, one feeder each
  • Hitters face away from each other · helmets on

How to Play

  • Feeders place balls; hitters rope line drives to the back of the cage
  • Feeder counts line drives only · re-loads only when safe
  • Swap roles after 1 minute · pair with most line drives after 2 min wins
  • Rotate so every pair competes
Coaching tip When the competition kicks in, kids rush. A faster bat is not a better hit. Solid contact comes from a swing that stays level, compact, and in control.
A5

Soft Toss Slugging

↑

Bridge from tee to live ball — solid swings on a moving target.

▶ Watch · 6:50

Setup

  • Line team up · coach 4 ft to the side of the plate
  • Bucket of balls · hitter in a strong, balanced stance

How to Play

  • Coach soft-tosses; hitter takes smooth, quality swings
  • 10 swings, then rotate
  • Each hitter tracks longest streak of solid hits · beat it next round
Coaching tip The #1 fix here is "don't pull your head out." Keep the head still through contact — eyes on the ball longer means more contact and better luck at the plate.
A6

Front Toss Frenzy

↑

Underhand tosses up the middle — build swing muscle memory.

▶ Watch · 8:02

Setup

  • Coach 15 ft from home, behind an L-screen
  • Bucket of balls · hitter in the cage with a bat

How to Play

  • Coach underhands a ball; hitter ropes a line drive up the middle
  • 10 swings per hitter · count the frozen ropes
  • Most line drives wins the round
Coaching tip Pitch recognition is not the goal here. Even on a toss that drifts, call out where it's headed so the hitter can focus 100% on a level swing and solid contact.
A7

Inside Outside

↑

Directional hitting — pull the inside pitch, push the outside one.

▶ Watch · 9:04

Setup

  • Two tees at home plate:
  • Inside tee — in front of the plate, hip height
  • Outside tee — on the outside corner, thigh height
  • Coach off to the side with a bucket of balls

How to Play

  • Hitter takes 6 cuts at the inside tee — aim between 2nd and 3rd
  • Then 6 at the outside tee — aim between 1st and 2nd
  • +1 every ball on target · improve the score next round
Coaching tip Different spots, same job: stay inside the ball and hit a line drive. Don't let them yank the outside pitch or push the inside one — direction comes from contact point, not muscle.
A8

Bounce Ball

↑

Patience & timing — wait on the ball before you commit.

▶ Watch · 10:17

Setup

  • Pick a few hitters · everyone else in the field
  • Coach stands between home and the mound
  • Bucket of foam balls

How to Play

  • Coach bounces the ball to land ~2 ft in front of the plate
  • Ball arrives at hip level for the hitter to swing
  • 10 swings each · track hits, beat the count next time
Coaching tip Keep weight on the back foot as long as possible before swinging. That's the same load you need to adjust from a fastball to a changeup — "when you're waiting for the express, it's hard to hit the locals."
A9

Think Fast

↑

Pitch recognition under pressure — pick the right ball mid-swing.

▶ Watch · 11:26

Setup

  • Line team up in foul territory
  • Coach 4 ft to the side of home with a bucket of two-color plastic balls
  • Hitter steps to the plate with a bat

How to Play

  • Soft-toss two different colored balls at the same time
  • While they're in the air, call out a color
  • Hitter adjusts mid-motion and hits the called ball
  • 5 swings each · most points after the round wins
Coaching tip Knowing what not to swing at matters just as much as the swing itself. This drill rehearses the split-second decision they'll make on every pitch come game day.
