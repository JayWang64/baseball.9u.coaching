01

Alligator Traps

↑

Gobble up grounders with proper two-hand technique.

▶ Watch · 0:11

Setup

  • Line the team up · coach stands 10 ft away
  • First player in set stance: knees bent, hips low

How to Play

  • Roll a grounder; fielder receives with both hands — top hand chomps like an alligator
  • Throw back, 3 grounders per player, then to the back of the line
  • Track clean fields — challenge them to beat the score next round
Coaching tip Eyes on the ball all the way into the glove. The better they see it, the less likely it is to scoot past them.
02

Belly Up

↑

Sharpen reaction time — react, charge, field, throw.

▶ Watch · 1:15

Setup

  • Line team up flat on their bellies
  • Coach stands 20 ft away with a ball

How to Play

  • Roll the ball toward the first player
  • On your call, they pop up, charge it, field it, throw it back
  • Work down the line · mix in a few pop-ups to keep them honest
Coaching tip Make them wait for the signal — no jumping the gun. In a real game the first step to fielding is seeing where the ball is going.
03

Lazy Catch

↑

Read grounders from a low knees-down stance.

▶ Watch · 2:21

Setup

  • Groups of 5 · coach 15 ft from the front of the line
  • All 5 players on both knees (don't sit on heels)
  • Glove fingertips on the ground, ready

How to Play

  • Roll a grounder to each player in turn
  • They field & throw back without standing or sitting
  • Work down the line, then back with backhanded grounders
  • +1 per clean field — beat last round's score
Coaching tip Two hands every time. Snap the top hand down on the ball like an alligator; on backhands, scoop and lift. One-handed pickups don't make it to the show.
04

Short Hop Showdown

↑

Dig the ball out of the dirt on a tough hop.

▶ Watch · 3:38

Setup

  • Pairs spaced 10 ft apart · one ball per pair
  • Both players start on their knees

How to Play

  • On your call, throw a short hop — bounce it a few feet in front of partner
  • Fielder scoops, returns it the same way
  • +1 per clean dig · first pair to 10 wins
  • Round two: same drill, but standing up
Coaching tip Anticipate the hop in a low athletic stance. Find the balance between being aggressive and meeting the ball with the glove — don't just wait for it.
05

Ground Ball Shuffle

↑

Side-shuffle to get the body in front of the ball.

▶ Watch · 4:41

Setup

  • Pairs standing 15 ft apart · one ball per pair

How to Play

  • Roll a grounder to one side of partner
  • Partner side-shuffles in front of the ball — no crossing feet
  • Field it & throw it back · swap roles after 5 grounders
  • Challenge: clean reps with perfect form, then do it cleaner
Coaching tip Shuffling and fielding at the same time is hard. Get them in a low athletic ready position before the ball arrives — then they can explode either way in the blink of an eye.
06

Pop Quiz

↑

Team fly-ball catches — call it, catch it, with both hands.

▶ Watch · 5:46

Setup

  • Team takes the field, a player at every position
  • Coach in front of home with a bucket of balls

How to Play

  • Set a target — e.g. 5 consecutive catches
  • Throw fly balls to random spots around the field
  • Drop one and the team resets to zero
  • Hit the target to win the round
Coaching tip Make them call the ball — loud. Even when there's no doubt who's getting it, calling the ball is the habit that prevents collisions in a real game.
07

Grab Bag

↑

Alternate grounders and pop-ups so they stay in ready position.

▶ Watch · 6:56

Setup

  • Two cones 10 ft apart in the outfield · team split behind each cone
  • Coach 15 ft in front, between the cones, with a bucket
  • Optional: a second coach at the other line

How to Play

  • Round 1: grounders to one line, pop-ups to the other
  • Field cleanly, throw back, move to the end of the other line
  • Round 2: mix grounders and pop-ups to both lines at random
  • +1 per clean field — beat last round's score
Coaching tip Foam balls tempt one-handed catches — don't let it slide. Reinforce two hands on every grounder and pop-up. That's how you stay on the field and off the blooper reel.
08

Force Out / Tag Out

↑

The two ways to record an out — read the call, make the play.

▶ Watch · 9:30

Setup

  • Pairs of one runner + one fielder (glove & ball)
  • Spread pairs across the base pads, 10 ft apart

How to Play

  • Call "tag out" — fielder must chase and tag the runner
  • Call "force out" — fielder runs to a base and steps on it before the runner
  • +1 per successful out · swap roles after ~60 seconds
Coaching tip The two outs confuse young players. Frame it this way: on the playground, tag means you're in; on the diamond, tag means you're out. Force = race the runner to the bag.
09

Pitcher's Fielding Practice

↑

PFP — the pitcher's other job: finish the pitch, then field the play.

▶ Watch · 10:44

Setup

  • Pitchers lined up by the mound
  • Coach at home with a fungo bat & bucket of balls
  • One fielder at 1st base to start

How to Play (three rounds)

  • Round 1 — cover 1st: pitch, then sprint to a spot ~10 ft up the line, turn, glove at chest, take the feed, touch the bag with the right foot
  • Round 2 — comebacker: hit it right back; pitcher sets feet, fields, makes a strong throw to 1st
  • Round 3 — double play: add a shortstop; pitcher fields and turns 1-6-3
  • +1 per clean field · highest score after 3 rounds wins
Coaching tip Finish every pitch in fielding position — feet and shoulders square to the plate, knees bent, athletic stance. A pitcher who can't field is only doing half the job.
